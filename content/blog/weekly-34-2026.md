-  Back to work after holidays

    I returned to work after 2 weeks of Summer holidays. I feel refreshed, but the
    use of AI also seems to be in my face more fiercely.

    A colleague seems to have taken the use of AI to the next level doing some kind
    of genetic algorithm-y programming by generating multiple exploratory solutions
    for a problem using agents and then trying to produce a next generation of
    solutions by selecting the best features of this one. This is super cool and
    something that wasn't possible before the current era of agentic programming,
    but it also raises questions about how theory building, collaboration, code
    reviews, knowledge sharing, etc should evolve.

    In this context, I found this post on why the author insists on [writing commit
    messages by hand](https://www.jvt.me/posts/2026/08/17/hand-write-commits/) even for AI generated code insightful.

    [Another article](https://amoffat.github.io/blog/vetting-burnout.html) that I found thought provoking is about how developing with
    LLMs can make you feel more burned out than if you built the comprehension as
    you wrote the code. It also comes with a nice UI with sliders to change
    different parameters and see how it impacts the developer's burnout factor.

-  Meeting inter-generational friends in Bengaluru

    We were able to meet a bunch of friends (and missed out on meeting some more)
    over the course of the week. We met a handful of kids of our friends ranging
    from the age of a month to 7 or 8 years old.

    It was fun to see Niya skate for the first time in a competition with a
    "proper" rink. It was overwhelming for her to see so many kids and parents and
    the rink and all the excitement there. But, luckily she had enough time to talk
    to some other kids and let her parents distract her and give her some words of
    encouragement. She ended up doing great and winning a gold medal in her
    category that she was so thrilled to win!

    I read the "Tiger who came to Tea" along with Aarush, while he was having
    lunch. My first surprise was him filling in the author's (Judith Kerr) name
    when I started reading from the cover page. And then he continue to verbatim
    complete lines as I continued to read. It was also amazing to see him ask his
    mom for a flight tracker showing where his dad currently was. His mom and
    grand-mom weren't sure where he learned about it's existence (they hadn't used
    it before, themselves). He probably extrapolated from cab rides or food
    delivery, but it was heart-warming to see such a tech-native/intuitive idea
    from a ~4 year old! And when it was time for me to leave, he wouldn't let me. He
    had a good time too, I guess.

    Addu caught me up on his football coaching and him having to miss a tournament
    for a family vacation during the Independence Day weekend. It sounded like he
    enjoyed the trek in Anantagiri, but would've preferred playing in the
    tournament over it. Also, briefly got to see how much work it is for their
    parents to keep Vismaya and Sharanya alive.

    With time, I hope they don't just remain kids of our friends, but we actually
    become inter-generational friends. :)

-  Bond Touch - Long-Distance Bracelet

    I learnt about [this long-distance bracelet](https://bond-touch.com/pages/bond-touch-lp) called Bond Touch from a friend,
    this week. It connects to your phone via bluetooth and lets you send a missing
    you "touch" to your loved one by simply touching your own band. It seems like a
    cool idea, given how difficult long-distance relationships can be. I wonder if
    people who wear fitness bands care to also wear another band like this!

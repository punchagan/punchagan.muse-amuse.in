-  Glorified Stochastic Parrots with powerful tools

    I enjoyed reading this [post by Ploum](https://ploum.net/2026-09-09-ai_policy.html) where they descibe their own personal AI
    policy -- essentially refusing to interact with anything produced using these
    Stochastic parrots and their creators. I've been seeing more and more posts
    like these, recently.

    > The direct consequence of my choice is that I will refuse to interact with
    > humans who take pride in making themselves a meat interface between me and the
    > algorithm they’ve chosen to replace their free will. '

    [Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/articles/2026-dont-like-llms.html) thinks out loud and shares his feelings about LLMs, on a similar
    note. He mentions that he choses not to interact with them, and seems to be
    particularly annoyed by their pretense to be human.

    > One of my most successful life-hacks is to avoid people I don’t like or don’t
    > trust. I decline to interact with them socially, and make a deliberate effort
    > to avoid working with them too, even if they are doing much that is beneficial.
    > I feel that hanging out with pleasant, capable people, the people with
    > integrity, has made my life a far better one. Hence my visceral dislike of
    > interacting with an LLM that’s not just making a pretense of being human, but
    > also posing as the kind of human I walk away from.

    A colleague (M) who has also recently started to use these AI via chat
    interfaces seemed tired of talking to bots that sound like arrogant humans.
    Another colleague (S) and I talked about this a few times, and last week he
    shared [this talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fB_lklm7xgU&t=1766s) exploring different ways that humans and AI could collaborate
    which had some cool ideas.

    I had the opportunity to learn about LLM harnesses a little bit last week, and
    it helped me see even more clearly how the "AI" that we are currently obsessed
    with is just glorified text prediction machines to which we, as a society,
    decided it's a good idea to give access to any and every tool out there! And
    then we make a hue and cry about them being a risk and breaching systems and
    attacking whatever it is that they are "attacking". The point Carl makes here
    about simply [treating AI like we treat other software](https://youtube.com/watch?v=9tr7Mby62bo) would be sufficient to
    put an end to a bunch of the alarm all around.

    Speaking of trusting them with access to anything and everything, it also blows
    my mind how comfortable we all seem to be to give away our data to these LLMs
    to let them save us some time. It seems like we haven't learnt anything from
    giving away our data carelessly to the previous edition of big-tech companies
    and their ad networks!

    Anyway, ...

    This [in-class rant](https://youtube.com/watch?v=N2a1J0UPeL4) by an IISc professor resonated with me. These are the kinds
    of questions I grapple with often these days.
